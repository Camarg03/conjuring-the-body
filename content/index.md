# Conjuring the Body

*Conjuring the Body* is a visual research archive on bodily fragments, medical imaging, data circulation, and digital presence.

The project investigates what happens when the body no longer appears only as a body, but as a scan, a file, a dataset, or a technical image. Medical images such as CT scans, MRIs, brain slices, and anatomical scans often begin in a clinical encounter, but they can later be stored, anonymized, copied, circulated, and connected to broader infrastructures of research, cloud storage, and artificial intelligence.

This archive follows that passage from body to image, from image to data, and from data to infrastructure. It asks how intimate bodily materials become searchable, comparable, governable, and trainable; who defines the conditions of their circulation; and what forms of presence, opacity, or vulnerability remain after abstraction.

> The body is not transformed into data only when it is scanned, but when it enters an infrastructure that can store it, classify it, circulate it, and make it trainable.

## Research lines

### Genealogies of anatomical visibility

This line follows older visual regimes through which the body was opened, mapped, diagrammed, staged, preserved, and transformed into anatomical knowledge. It connects historical illustration, dissection, wax models, vascular preparations, and the digital atlas of the Visible Human Project.

- [[The body as anatomical territory]]
- [[Anatomical illustration - seven historical body images]]
- [[Visible Human Project - informatic body and digital anatomy]]

### Datasets and corpora

This line studies open medical image repositories as infrastructures where bodily fragments become research material. These entries focus on how images are grouped, de-identified, annotated, labeled, paired with reports, and made available for machine learning.

- [[The Cancer Imaging Archive - heterogeneous cancer image collections]]
- [[MIMIC-CXR - radiographs and reports as multimodal medical data]]
- [[WORC Database - Rotterdam radiomics corpus]]

### Infrastructure, opacity, and governance

This line follows the technical and political workflows through which medical images become operational data: cloud services, storage systems, health data platforms, opt-out settings, black boxes, and the governance of sensitive bodily information.

- [[AWS medical AI workflow]]
- [[Black box medical imaging]]
- [[Opt-out as default consent]]

### Bias, fairness, and machine-readable traces

This line examines what medical AI can infer from images beyond conventional human interpretation. It focuses on dataset composition, demographic generalization, radiomics fairness, race as a machine-readable signal, and the possibility that medical images retain biometric traces after de-identification.

This line documents practical workflows for converting medical or anatomical image sequences into volumes, point clouds, renders, meshes, or other visual structures. These notes are not diagnostic manuals; they track how bodily images are technically reassembled and transformed.
